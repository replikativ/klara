# Klara challenge 2: hosting, proof staging, and the price of a live world

Reviewed 2026-10-27. **Recommendation: retain JVM hosting, but make optimization dispensable and keep proof environments immutable.** Lean-hosted Klara is technically credible; it loses primarily on the existing application boundary, not on an inability to express dynamic languages. The largest immediate risks are proof validity across redefinition, mandatory semantic re-expansion, and retention of obsolete worlds/classes.

Severity: **Critical** threatens certification soundness; **High** threatens a central product guarantee; **Medium** requires substantial implementation work; **Low** is a qualification. I read all four design documents and the previous review. Design references are relative to `lambda-join/doc/klara/`; other repository-prefixed references are relative to `/home/christian-weilbach/Development/`. Inspection used the working trees, including existing changes; Lean HEAD was `55f80e10a3`. Timings below are repository reports, not fresh benchmarks. Disk usage is a fresh read-only observation. Proposed thresholds are acceptance criteria, not achieved results.

## 1. Should Klara extend Lean 4 instead?

**Verdict — Medium architectural risk: JVM-first is justified, conditional on proving the integration advantage. Keep native Lean as an executable specification and competing prototype.** “Inherit the JVM's runtime and Lean's logic” describes a promising division of labor, but ansatz is another implementation of that logic, not an automatic inheritance of Lean's entire trust and tooling ecosystem.

### What Lean actually supplies

Lean offers a kernel, dependent elaboration, syntax extensions, in-language metaprogramming, a large mathematical library, native compilation, and a community already producing proof tools. AI momentum is concrete: AlphaProof used Lean for formal mathematical reasoning, with IMO 2024 results documented by its authors. That is evidence for reusable proof infrastructure and training investment, not evidence that mathematical tactics solve effect analysis. [AlphaProof research report](https://research.google/pubs/olympiad-level-formal-mathematical-reasoning-with-reinforcement-learning/).

Its runtime is a serious alternative for persistent data. Exact reference counts enable destructive reuse when uniqueness is available; Lean's reset/reuse machinery appears directly in `lean4/src/Lean/Compiler/IR.lean:36–42`. Calling this simply “Perceus” blurs related implementations: Lean's foundational account is *Counting Immutable Beans*; Perceus is a related RC compilation approach. Neither gives free reuse after a fork when both branches retain the same node. [Counting Immutable Beans](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05647), [Lean reference counting](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Run-Time-Code/Reference-Counting/).

The concrete runtime audit changes several common objections:

- **Environment:** checked declaration admission rejects an existing name (`lean4/src/kernel/environment.cpp:106–109`), while environments are persistent values (`lean4/src/Lean/Environment.lean:30–35`). “Append-only” accurately describes checked extension within one lineage, not an inability to branch or retain earlier environments. This is already close to Klara's immutable definition versions.
- **Evaluation:** `#eval` elaborates a temporary declaration and invokes `evalConst` (`lean4/src/Lean/Elab/BuiltinEvalCommand.lean:173–219`). The IR interpreter uses stack slots and can call native boxed entry points discovered by symbol lookup (`lean4/src/library/ir_interpreter.cpp:7–26`). Lean is not restricted to rebuilding an executable before evaluating new code.
- **Loading:** `loadDynlib` and `loadPlugin` exist. Their documented policy is never to unload libraries/plugins and to reject overlapping symbols (`lean4/src/Lean/LoadDynlib.lean:56–90`). This is extensibility, not a complete hot-reload lifecycle.
- **Compiler:** LCNF has base, monomorphization, impure lowering, SCC splitting, and IR compilation (`lean4/src/Lean/Compiler/LCNF/Main.lean:107–125`). C emission is integrated; LLVM emitter/binding modules also exist, but their imports explicitly say they are not required by the compiler (`lean4/src/Lean/Compiler/IR.lean:18–30`). Neither their presence nor the interpreter's JIT comment establishes a production adaptive JIT with deoptimization.
- **Concurrency:** `Task.spawn`, `map`, and `bind`, including thread-pool priorities, are implemented (`lean4/src/Init/Core.lean:617–719`). Tasks supply scheduling, not Klara's forkable control state, authority transfer, or replay semantics.

An untyped object language does not require an untyped implementation language. Define `Value`, syntax, closures, machine frames, worlds, and errors in Lean; use a fuelled total step relation for reasoning and an executable driver for unrestricted computation. Lean even supplies a `Dynamic` container (`lean4/src/Init/Dynamic.lean:62–108`), although Klara-specific tagged values would give a clearer serialization and authority model. Optional Klara proofs can concern subsets of these values without forcing users into dependent types.

### What a Lean-hosted Klara would cost

The sensible design preserves Lean's kernel: assign every definition a fresh internal identity and maintain a separate persistent map from user names to versions. Fibers hold that map. A CEK-style interpreter executes generic Klara immediately; a compiler generates fresh Lean/LCNF declarations for hot fragments. A basis-and-guard dispatch layer selects compiled entries. Old entries remain usable by old fibers. No theorem constant changes meaning.

This buys one proof ecosystem and native RC, but leaves major work: a Lisp frontend; dynamic dispatch; effect/continuation lowering; profiling; compilation scheduling; guards and state reconstruction; source debugging; version reclamation; and resource semantics. Native plugins with fresh symbols could bootstrap compilation, but permanent library retention would eventually make repeated reloads expensive. Safe unloading needs ownership of every executable pointer and a quiescence protocol, or disposable worker processes. An LLVM JIT would still need these policies.

The decisive disadvantage is the application boundary. Klara's stated requirement is shared Clojure values and native IFn calls, including datahike (`00-design-space.md:39–43`; `01-core-v1.md:155–170`). A native Lean host needs JNI/object handles, marshaling, or a service boundary. Each choice weakens fine-grained collection interoperability or complicates two memory-management systems. A Lean-generated JVM backend would preserve this boundary, but is itself a large compiler/runtime project. Coarse datahike snapshot exchange may be acceptable for native numerical workers; it is a different proposition from embedding into an existing Clojure application.

Conversely, JVM-first pays for ansatz kernel/elaborator maintenance and artifact translation. Imported kernel declarations do not reproduce Lean macros, executable tactics, or persistent extensions: ansatz explicitly documents this limitation and mismatched frontend cohorts (`ansatz/doc/stores.md:81–106`). Mathlib import is valuable, but not equivalent to running the current Mathlib development environment unchanged.

**Falsification experiment:** give JVM and Lean prototypes the same bounded project budget and three workloads: forked SMC, a live datahike-backed application with callbacks, and an edit/prove/run loop. Require identical traces and old/new-world coexistence. Measure engineering time, cold startup, edit-to-result p99, throughput, peak retained memory, and boundary copying. Reconsider JVM-first if Lean delivers materially better integrated performance—provisionally 2× throughput or half the retained heap—while meeting the same reload budget and integration requirements. Reconsider ansatz-first independently if native Lean checking/export takes less maintenance and latency than maintaining the imported frontend cohort. A native proof service with a JVM runtime is a valid third option.

## 2. Can Klara be hosted efficiently?

**Verdict — High risk until measured: efficient hosting is plausible for ordinary calls and bounded effect regions; blanket zero overhead is not.** The relevant comparison is Clojure with equivalent behavior, plus explicit charges for semantics Clojure does not provide.

Clojure already pays for function objects, generic object interfaces, and normally a Var dereference before invocation. Direct linking replaces that lookup with a static call and deliberately stops observing ordinary redefinitions; `^:dynamic` and `^:redef` preserve indirection. This is an excellent dev/collapse precedent, but it also establishes that dynamism has a cost even before Klara adds worlds. HotSpot may optimize particular stable calls; IFn is not a fixed nanosecond tariff. [Clojure compilation reference](https://clojure.org/reference/compilation#directlinking).

| Mechanism | Structural requirement | Avoidable implementation cost |
|---|---|---|
| `perform`/`handle` | Locate the applicable handler; retain resumable state when suspension occurs | Map construction per operation, universal CPS, allocating frames for tail-resumptive operations |
| Basis reads | Resolve according to the caller's specified world | Persistent-map lookup on every invocation; repeated hashing of an unchanged dependency closure |
| Authority | Prevent unauthorized consume/settle under aliases and concurrency | Checking every scalar; wrapping all data; global contention for unrelated resources |
| Sort classification | Establish the reachable resource footprint at relevant boundaries | Re-scanning native immutable aggregates whose summaries can be maintained compositionally |
| Receipts | Record requested observations and provenance | Allocating receipt objects when no ledger is active; retaining unbounded histories |
| Specialization | Validate assumptions before optimized execution | Global invalidation walks, mandatory recompilation, unbounded variants |

Non-suspending effects can lower to direct handler calls or explicit capability arguments. Suspensions require explicit frames/selective CPS, and multi-shot execution additionally requires copying or replaying control state. A host callback cannot magically capture an intervening untransformed Java/Clojure stack. Define an opaque boundary or a dedicated adapter. The previous review's objection remains (`reviews/codex-astra-critic.md:51–61`); renaming spindel replay “multi-shot” does not remove replay cost or repeated-I/O hazards.

The existing stack supplies useful components. Forkable SCI already isolates world cells and exposes custom value-forking policy (`sci/src/sci/core.cljc:415–443`). Spindel has explicit settlement authority (`spindel/src/org/replikativ/spindel/yggdrasil.cljc:484–542`). Raster already loads emitted bytecode using compilation classloaders (`raster/src/raster/compiler/backend/jvm/bytecode.clj:26–39,914`). These are credible starting points, not evidence that their composition has bounded overhead. In particular, a forked interpreter environment is not yet a snapshot of every suspended computation and external effect.

**Design rule:** resolve names into a basis-specific execution context, cache small call-site target sets, and fall back to generic resolution on an entry guard failure. Preserve old entries rather than globally patching them to the newest implementation. For arbitrary imported maps, first-time deep classification can be linear in reachable data; classify that as admission cost, not O(1) fork cost. For native aggregates, maintain immutable footprint summaries. Charge authority checks to authority operations; erase them only under a proof that covers aliases and asynchronous interference.

Receipts deserve two budgets. The design promises no allocation outside `with-ledger` and ≤2% overhead (`01-core-v1.md:174–178`). Test that directly with allocation profiling. Inside the ledger, use bounded buffers or streaming storage, and account for hashing, serialization, and retention. A compiler receipt per installed artifact is different from a receipt per arithmetic operation. Conflating them makes the hot path unnecessarily expensive.

**Experiment:** compare plain Clojure, generic Klara, specialized Klara, and ledger-enabled Klara on direct calls, monomorphic and megamorphic dispatch, tail effects, suspensions, host callbacks, and SMC. Report throughput, allocations/op, frame bytes/particle, first-fork scans, and p99 pauses. Use the design's ≤1.1× delegate and ≤1.2× protocol targets for the corresponding fast paths (`01-core-v1.md:238–240`), not for continuation capture or durable branching. Reject a design that makes pure ordinary calls allocate continuation frames; reconsider effect lowering if capture/retention dominates the real simulation despite specialization.

## 3. Will staging and specialization keep reload cheap?

**Verdict — High risk: the mitigations can prevent compulsory optimization storms, but do not yet guarantee a Clojure-quality REPL.** Separate publication latency, time to first correct new-world result, steady-state slowdown, and retained memory. A prompt that returns immediately before the next call spends seconds rebuilding is not cheap reload.

Belyakova et al.'s OOPSLA 2020 *World Age in Julia* provides a semantics of fixed method-table execution and proves optimization correctness, including inlining and direct calls. World age enables optimization despite `eval`; it does not itself cause recompilation storms. The invalidation problem arises when compiled assumptions cease to hold for later worlds. [World-age paper, §§4.5–4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.07516).

Julia's invalidation account demonstrates that adding methods can invalidate inferred return types and absence-of-method assumptions, not merely previously selected callees. It also documents substantial reductions between Julia 1.5 and the developing 1.6. Treat this as a dependency-design lesson rather than a permanent verdict on Julia performance. [Julia invalidation analysis](https://julialang.org/blog/2020/08/invalidations/).

**Low — Update the historical premise.** Current Revise documentation, generated October 25, 2026, places inability to revise structs under “Prior to Julia 1.12”; explicit struct revision is now supported. It still documents missing implicit binding dependencies, macro/generated-function propagation limitations, and old-world long-running tasks. The local `Revise.jl/docs/src/limitations.md:1–11` describes the older restriction. Pin versions when comparing; neither documentation establishes automatic migration of every existing object. [Current Revise limitations](https://timholy.github.io/Revise.jl/stable/limitations/).

### Assessment of each mitigation

**Pinned worlds — necessary, insufficient.** `01-core-v1.md:127–131` correctly separates meaning-changing expansion from transparent optimization. But fork ancestry is a graph, not Julia's single increasing age. Validity must follow exact dependency identities, not a scalar “new enough” test. Define whether a captured function uses its creation basis or caller basis. Basis raising needs a quiescent language boundary with compatible continuation state; “next safe point” (`02-examples.md:75–90`) cannot promise interruption of arbitrary host calls.

**Generic fallback — strongest protection, with a qualification.** Every accepted definition needs executable generic semantics independent of optional proof, inference, and specialization. Entry guards can simply choose generic code. Mid-body deoptimization must reconstruct source state; restarting after a write duplicates effects. Initially allow swaps only at entry or explicit transferable-state boundaries. Wandler's adaptive driver follows this restricted pattern and explicitly requires state compatibility; its swap API checks no certificates (`wandler/src/wandler/jit/swap.clj:1–7,40–79`).

The qualification is decisive: a generic interpreter cannot rescue stale *meaning*. If redefining a macro means callers re-expand in the new world, their new executable syntax must be produced before execution. An arbitrary macro may diverge or perform expensive authorized work. Automatic semantic propagation is more work than Clojure's ordinary manual re-evaluation workflow. No cache policy can guarantee equal latency for this strictly stronger behavior. Offer explicit expansion transactions, bounded/cancellable demand, and a visible pending/error state; never silently run old expansion while claiming the new basis.

**Early cutoff — effective only at the right dependency boundary.** An unchanged interface permits callers using indirection to survive; it does not validate callers that inlined the body. An unchanged expansion hash cuts off downstream work only after the expansion's inputs have been validated. Record separate dependencies on interface, body, expansion, dispatch winner, negative lookup, layout, and proof environment. The prototype still compares only winning patterns when retaining cached implementations (`lambda-join/v0/src/v0/dispatch.clj:101–108,154–178`). Replacing a body at the same pattern is the obvious adversarial test; this remains an inspection-derived defect, not a fresh execution result.

**Dev/collapse modes — make the contract explicit.** In dev mode, open definition boundaries retain indirection and bounded local specialization. Collapse may inline a closed dependency manifest aggressively. A collapsed artifact either stays pinned, falls back through a separately retained generic implementation, or is rebuilt explicitly; it cannot promise unrestricted live updates and unrestricted whole-program assumptions simultaneously. This restriction should appear in the module interface and debugger.

**Asynchronous specialization — necessary, not sufficient.** Compilation competes for CPU, memory, GC, class verification, and locks even on a background thread. Use bounded queues, cancellation/coalescing by definition version, per-definition variant caps, and low-priority resource budgets. Never make definition publication wait for optimizer locks or reverse-dependency traversal. Lazy validation must also be budgeted: if proving a cached artifact valid requires walking a huge graph, skip it and execute generic code.

**Shape evolution — version representation separately from names.** Standard JVM TI redefinition cannot add/remove fields or methods, change signatures, or change inheritance; active frames continue old bytecode, and existing objects are not migrated. [JVM TI 25, RedefineClasses](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/25/docs/specs/jvmti.html#RedefineClasses), [Instrumentation API](https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/25/docs/api/java.instrument/java/lang/instrument/Instrumentation.html#redefineClasses(java.lang.instrument.ClassDefinition...)).

Clojure-style fresh classloading avoids in-place layout mutation but leaves old and new `defrecord` instances with distinct class identities. Raster already contains a class-hierarchy fallback for records loaded by different classloaders (`raster/src/raster/compiler/backend/jvm/bytecode.clj:35–39`). Klara should use logical shape IDs plus immutable schema versions, generic field lookup, and explicit optional migrations. Specialized field access guards the exact layout. Old values remain inspectable; new code accepts them through a declared compatibility adapter or reports a schema error. Never equate a printable class name with layout identity.

### Measurable “never worse” obligations

An unconditional wall-clock guarantee is impossible on a shared JVM, and automatic macro propagation adds semantic work absent from the baseline. Specify a comparable fragment: ordinary function/dispatch edits, unchanged supported host ABI, bounded source expansion, no user-requested migration, and no optional optimization requirement. Use the same JDK, heap, workload, machine, and warmup; compare Clojure with direct linking disabled. Treat the following as release gates:

1. **R1, publication:** after required parsing/expansion, publishing an edit performs work bounded by the changed definitions and namespace-map update, independent of specialization count and reverse-dependency fanout. Instrument visits; test 10, 1,000, and 100,000 dependents. Optional optimization jobs awaited: exactly zero.
2. **R2, first correct result:** benchmark publication-to-first-result separately from prompt return. Proposed p99 gate: at most Clojure's matched operation plus `max(5 ms, 10%)`. Test cold caches, optimizer disabled, proof worker stalled, and a deliberately saturated background queue. Failing this gate forbids the “never worse” claim.
3. **R3, availability:** deleting every specialization cache must preserve results and effect traces. Failed guards never repeat I/O; compiler/prover timeout never prevents execution of an otherwise accepted unannotated definition. Checked annotation failure remains a language error, not speculative deoptimization.
4. **R4, isolation:** two pinned fibers and two sibling forks execute old/new macro and method versions concurrently. Include same-pattern replacement, deletion, new more-specific methods, negative lookups, cancellation, and callback re-entry. Compare traces to a no-optimization reference machine.
5. **R5, bounded cache cost:** cap variants, queued jobs, proof-cache bytes, and validation work. High-churn sites become permanently generic until a cooldown expires. Report maximum foreground validation work and throughput under continuous editing, not just median cache-hit time.
6. **R6, retirement:** after 10,000 edits with bounded live bases, release old fibers and inspect heap, metaspace, classloader reachability, and dependency indexes. Require a plateau within a declared cache budget; explicitly retained snapshots are accounted separately. A fresh loader per edit is insufficient if its parent chain or a static registry retains all predecessors.
7. **R7, shape continuity:** change fields/types repeatedly while retaining old records, closures, and suspended frames. Printing, generic access, migration errors, and new construction remain usable without restart. Test nominal Java interop separately as a restricted ABI boundary.
8. **R8, expansion honesty:** an unchanged expansion stops downstream invalidation; a changed or nonterminating expansion never masquerades as current. Cancellation preserves the prior usable basis. Report semantic expansion cost separately and require explicit publication/adoption for unbounded generators.

These obligations can establish bounded architectural work and measured latency parity for a stated fragment. They cannot turn richer semantics into a universal performance theorem.

## 4. Can ansatz prove away handlers and dispatch?

**Verdict — High potential, Critical trust-boundary requirement: use proof staging for small closed facts, never as a mandatory whole-program elaboration step.** A type checker checks a proposed fact; it does not automatically discover useful effect summaries or justify arbitrary Java behavior.

Represent a candidate rewrite and its premises in the kernel: a handler-free trace fragment, a uniquely selected method under a frozen table, a nonescaping authority, or a layout-preserving representation transformation. Prove a reusable theorem once and instantiate it cheaply. Erasing a handler requires equivalence of observable traces, including exceptions, suspension, cancellation, receipts, and host effects—not merely equality of returned integers. A proof about a fixed dispatch table remains conditional on that exact table and argument guard.

Wandler demonstrates the outline: it certifies expression rewrites, compiles a plan, and supports strict replay that rechecks expressions/proofs and ignores supplied executable closures (`wandler/src/wandler/jit/pgo.clj:1–7,22–69`). The file explicitly leaves code generation trusted. Klara must bridge source effects to proof IR and proof IR to emitted bytecode through a validated translation or a clearly documented trusted compiler. A theorem about the wrong encoding eliminates no real risk.

**Critical — Surface redefinition must not mutate the meaning of proof constants.** Ansatz exposes `check-constant-replace` for REPL definitions (`ansatz/src/ansatz/kernel/env.clj:112–119`). Its implementation checks the incoming declaration and replaces the same-named entry; `Env.addOrReplaceConstant` retains the other entries (`ansatz/src-java/ansatz/kernel/TypeChecker.java:2573–2585`; `Env.java:179–186`). This does not establish validity of previously admitted dependents under the replacement.

The adversarial case is `f := 0`, then a theorem `h : f = 0`, then redefining `f := 1`. Checking the new `f` alone cannot preserve the old theorem's justification in the resulting environment. This is a concrete admission-boundary risk inferred from code, not a claimed executed exploit of every surface path. Before trusting staging certificates, test it through the actual public API. Require fresh internal names, immutable proof-environment roots, and dependency-closure identities; alternatively remove/recheck every affected dependent before admitting that environment. Kernel install paths being strict does not repair a mixed environment after replacement. This requirement strengthens the case for borrowing Lean's admission discipline even while retaining Clojure-style user names.

### Compilation overhead is already visible

Repository evidence gives useful bounds on expectations:

- `ansatz/README.md:232–258` reports roughly 3 seconds for cold zero-config readiness; full Mathlib first sessions take 47.4–49.1 seconds with the library jar versus 12.5–13.5 seconds with an application uberjar, on one machine with warm page cache. Warm lookup is reported near 3 µs, but `simp` at 50–100 ms. Lookup latency is not elaboration latency.
- `ansatz/README.md:266–275` reports a roughly 1.3 GiB download and 5 GB unpacked Mathlib store. Fresh `du -sh` observations here found **173 MiB** allocated for `ansatz/test-data/init-store`, **96 MiB** for `init.ndjson`, and **3.2 MiB** for `init-medium.ndjson`. These are disk footprints, not heap/startup measurements; the README's “10MB” medium-fixture description at line 375 differs from this checkout.
- Wandler reports lazy Init opening around 40 ms and cached law rechecking around 20 ms (`wandler/docs/TUTORIAL.md:47–64`). Its actual cache saves proved constants and rechecks them, with unresolved entries falling back to proving (`wandler/src/wandler/laws/faq.clj:460–509`). Fast opening does not include all first-use loading/proving.
- Prepared stores do not check every imported proof at startup (`ansatz/doc/stores.md:70–74`). Artifact hashes, checker identity, dependency cohort, and admission policy therefore belong in certification provenance.

**Experiment:** stage three facts—no suspension, fixed-table dispatch, and a handler-fusion rewrite—against 1,000 edited definitions. Measure cold loading, elaboration/search, proof checking, bytecode generation, and installation separately, with cache hits and adversarial misses. Start with a 50 ms background proof budget per candidate, bounded memory, and generic fallback on timeout; make larger proofs explicit build tasks. Key caches by canonical proposition, dependency bodies, checker/version/cohort, ABI, and transformation identity. Break-even requires expected remaining executions times saved cost to exceed total optimization cost. In dev mode, compilation churn reduces that horizon sharply.

The immediate deliverable should be one fork-and-edit simulation that passes R1–R8 with the prover disabled, then gains a measured speedup when proof staging is enabled. That would substantiate the runtime/logic split far more convincingly than either importing more Mathlib or committing to a second native runtime.
